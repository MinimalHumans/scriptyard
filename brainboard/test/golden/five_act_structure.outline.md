# Five Act Structure

## Act 1 — Act One : Setup

> This act introduces the main characters, world, and central premise. It establishes the status quo and presents the inciting incident that disrupts it. By the end, the protagonist commits to a course of action, setting the main story in motion.

- **Shift:** What changes by the end...

## Act 2 — Act Two : Rising Action

> The protagonist begins pursuing their goal but encounters early obstacles and complications. Stakes start to build, and relationships and conflicts deepen. This act often ends with a turning point that raises the tension significantly.

## Act 3 — Act Three : Climax (Midpoint)

> The story reaches a major turning point or revelation that changes the direction of the narrative. This could be a victory, defeat, or major discovery, but it reframes the protagonist’s understanding of the conflict and raises the stakes further.

## Act 4 — Act Four : Complications

> Consequences of the midpoint unfold, and things begin to spiral or intensify toward a breaking point. The protagonist faces their greatest challenges, often leading to a low moment where success seems out of reach.

## Act 5 — Act Five : Resolution

> The protagonist confronts the central conflict in the climax and resolves it. The story then winds down, showing the aftermath and how the characters or world have changed.
